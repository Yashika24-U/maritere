What is lifestyle medicine?

Lifestyle Medicine is a medical specialty that uses therapeutic lifestyle changes—like nutrition, exercise, sleep, stress management, social connection, and avoiding risky substances—as the primary treatment to prevent, manage, and even reverse chronic disease.




You Have More Control Than You Think

The science behind lifestyle choices and disease prevention

Chronic disease is the leading cause of death and disability in the United States — but the majority of it is preventable. Small, consistent changes in your daily habits can dramatically reduce your risk of serious illness and improve your long-term health.
Evidence shows that small, consistent changes in your daily habits can dramatically improve your long-term health.

6 in 10 adults in the U.S. have a chronic disease

Up to 80% of chronic diseases can be prevented through healthy eating, regular physical activity, avoiding tobacco, and maintaining a healthy weight.

4 in 10 adults have two or more chronic diseases

Combining a healthy diet, regular exercise, and not smoking can reduce your risk of multiple chronic illnesses by about 78%.

33% of deaths are due to heart disease and stroke

Heart disease and stroke risk can be lowered by up to 80% through lifestyle choices such as physical activity, a healthy diet, not smoking, weight management, and minimal to no alcohol use.

1.7 million people are diagnosed with cancer every year

At least 40% of cancers are preventable through lifestyle habits like avoiding tobacco, healthy eating patterns, physical activity, and maintaining a healthy weight.

Alzheimer’s is the 6th leading cause of death

Up to 40% of dementia cases may be preventable or delayed by lifestyle factors such as exercise, social engagement, healthy diet, and managing conditions like high blood pressure and diabetes.

37 million Americans have diabetes

The risk of developing type 2 diabetes can be reduced by up to 90% through healthy lifestyle habits — especially a healthy weight, regular movement, balanced nutrition, and not smoking.

1 in 7 adults has chronic kidney disease (CKD)

Healthy lifestyle behaviors can reduce CKD risk by about 68%, particularly through managing blood pressure, maintaining a healthy weight, eating well, and staying physically active.

42% of U.S. adults live with obesity

Lifestyle changes like improved nutrition, physical activity, and behavioral support can prevent obesity and reduce the risk of related conditions (like diabetes and heart disease) by 50–80%.

Why it Matters?

Every day, your choices — what you eat, how you move, how you rest, how you manage stress — directly shape your health. Lifestyle Medicine gives you the tools to reclaim control of your well-being, one sustainable habit at a time.
